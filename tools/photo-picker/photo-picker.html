MVP Media Photo Picker

Runs entirely in this browser window. Your photos are never uploaded anywhere; the tool only saves files to your Downloads folder (or a folder you choose).

0Convert RAW to JPEG (if you shot RAW)

  1. Choose a folder (subfolders included) or individual files. Works with Canon CR2/CR3, Nikon NEF, Sony ARW, Adobe DNG, Fujifilm RAF, Olympus ORF, Panasonic RW2, Samsung SRW, and Pentax PEF.
  2. Press Convert to JPEG. Each photo keeps its file name (IMG_1234.CR3 becomes IMG_1234.jpg) and its rotation, with location and camera data removed.
  3. Then use the converted folder in steps 1 and 2.

How it works: every RAW file carries a full-size JPEG the camera made when you took the shot, and the tool saves that (the same picture as the camera's own JPEG setting, with its color style). It does not re-develop the sensor data the way Lightroom does. Some cameras, mostly older Sony models and phone DNGs, only store a smaller copy; the tool tells you when that happens.

1Make contact sheets

  1. Choose the folder that holds your photos. Subfolders are included, and each subfolder (shoot) gets its own sheets.
  2. Press Make contact sheets. Every photo gets a number printed under its thumbnail.
  3. Zip files land in your Downloads folder, each under 24 MB. Send them to Claude one at a time.

2Pick, auto-edit, and export

  1. Choose the same folder as in step 1, so the numbers match.
  2. Choose the photos, any of three ways: paste the numbers Claude picked (ranges like 120-135 work), press Auto-pick the best, or press Review photos and tap the ones you want. You can mix them.
  3. Press Export picks. Each photo is automatically edited (color cast removed, brightness and contrast balanced, lightly sharpened; the Editorial setting also adds the MVP magazine finish), cropped if you choose a shape, resized, and saved as a web-ready JPEG with location and camera data removed, in zips under 24 MB.

Auto-pick groups burst shots of the same pose and keeps the sharpest, best-exposed frame of each group. It judges technical quality only (focus, exposure, duplicates), not taste, so look over the picks in Review.

Works with JPEG, PNG, and WebP in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari. iPhone HEIC photos work in Safari on a Mac; in other browsers, export them as JPEG first. Camera RAW files (CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, DNG, and others) are skipped in steps 1 and 2: convert them in step 0 first. If the browser asks to allow multiple downloads, choose Allow.
